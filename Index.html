About me

I didn't find design.
I fell into it.

After graduating with a BE in Computer Engineering, I spent months trying to become a full-stack developer. I built things in HTML, CSS, JavaScript, React. The results never matched the effort. So instead of grinding harder at something that wasn't aligning, I stopped and looked around.

Explored cloud, Linux, data analytics. Nothing aligned. Then I found UI/UX design — and realised I'd been doing pieces of it my whole life. Back in diploma, I was always the one deciding how the screens should look, how the flows should work. I just didn't know that was a job.

"Once I found the field, I stopped waiting for permission to start."

Four projects later — including a real internship, research with actual users, and a redesign my mom tested herself — I bring a first-principles approach to every product decision I make.

How I think

I don't start with screens.
I start with behavior.

Every project I've done follows the same pattern — not as a ritual, but because every time I skipped a step, the design suffered for it. This is how I work through a problem.

01
Observe
Notice a real friction — not a trend, not a brief. A moment where someone struggled.
02
Investigate
Talk to people. Map the existing experience. Test my own assumptions first.
03
Define
Separate symptoms from causes. The real problem is rarely what it looks like on the surface.
04
Design
Wireframes before visuals. Architecture before aesthetics. Structure before colour.
05
Validate
Put it in front of real users. They tell you what you missed.
The most important design decision I ever made was not opening Figma.
On the Library App I spent a week assuming I knew the problem. A single survey corrected me. The redesign that followed was more focused and more useful — because it solved the right thing.

DOX — Task Management Tool

"Every tool drowned me in features. I designed one that doesn't."

Built during my internship at Amdox Technologies. I started by using Jira, ClickUp, Linear, and Trello as a first-time user — and immediately felt the problem. Too many features, no clear path forward. DOX fixes that.

UTS App Redesign

"My mom asked me to help her book a ticket. She couldn't do it alone. That became this."

The UTS app has millions of daily users — but people like my mom rely on others to use it. After talking to her and my neighbours, I found the real problem: it wasn't a bad UI. It was fear of making irreversible mistakes.

→ Primary research — interviewed real non-tech users, not guessed their problems
→ New feature designed — first-time video booking guide popup, not just a visual polish
→ FAB button for re-accessing guide — existing users aren't left behind
→ Decluttered homepage — reduced visual anxiety for first-time users
Key lesson Fear is a UX problem, not a UI one.

Library App Redesign

"I assumed the problem. The users corrected me. That was the most important lesson I learned."

My first UX project — a self-initiated redesign to practice the full process. I thought bad UI was driving users away. A survey told me I was wrong. The real issue was irrelevant search results caused by a broken algorithm.

→ Assumption proven wrong — started with a UI hypothesis, research revealed the real issue
→ User survey conducted — real data over designer intuition
→ Redesigned search experience — relevance filters, priority ranking, clearer results
→ 32% increase in purchases — post-redesign metric improvement tracked in case study
Key lesson Never assume the problem. Research it.

Design principles

Three things every
project taught me.

01
Assumptions expire. Research doesn't.

Every project I've done has invalidated at least one assumption I walked in with. The Library App taught me this first — I assumed the problem was the UI. It was the search algorithm. I now treat every assumption as a hypothesis to be disproved, not confirmed.

From: Library App
02
Complexity should be earned, not inherited.

When I used Jira and ClickUp for the first time, I felt the cognitive load before I understood the features. DOX was built on one belief: a new user should never see a feature they didn't ask for yet. Complexity is a reward for mastery, not a welcome mat.

From: DOX
03
Fear is a UX problem, not a UI one.

My mom knew what she wanted to do. She had the app open. She didn't book the ticket because she was afraid of making an irreversible mistake. That's not a colour or layout problem. Good UX gives users the courage to act.

From: UTS Redesign
